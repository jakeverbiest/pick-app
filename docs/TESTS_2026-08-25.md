Both run on what is already on your phone (builds 28–31 + today's OTAs). No new
build needed. **Force-quit Pick twice before the first walk** so today's updates
are actually applied.

---

## The trick that makes these easy

Yesterday's problem was never the walking, it was that **20 picks at 10-second
spacing blur together**. 39 counted events against a bare total of 20 could mean
"every pick counted twice" or "twelve double-counts plus fifteen false
positives", and clustering them gave a different answer at every threshold I
tried (26 clusters at 3s, 20 at 5s, 17 at 6s). Nothing could separate the two.

**Fix: space the picks so far apart that they cannot blur.** At 20 seconds
apart, every burst of detections in the log belongs to exactly one pick, at any
threshold. The isolation does the work that timestamps would have done — so
there is nothing to log, nothing to transcribe, and no stopwatch.

## Walk 1 — B7, isolated picks

**Question: how many counts does ONE pick produce?**

- Phone in your pocket. Normal walking pace.
- **10 picks, 20 seconds of walking between each.** Count "one-one-thousand" to
  twenty, or just walk about 25 paces — it does not need to be exact, it needs
  to be *wide*.
- Full stop for each pick. Bend, ~2 seconds down and up, as if bagging it.
- After pick 10, **keep walking 10 more seconds**, then stop and save.
  (Anything detected in the last 6 seconds is discarded by the pocket-removal
  guard — stop too soon and you lose pick 10 for a protocol reason.)
- **Do not correct the count** when you save.

About 4 minutes. Send the JSON and confirm it was 10 picks.

## Walk 2 — A9, zero picks

**Question: how many false positives are left after the median fix?**

- Same pocket, same pace. **Zero picks, zero stops.**
- Walk a **full 4 minutes** — this one measures a *rate*, so the clock is the
  sample size. Do not cut it short.
- Save without correcting.

Pick a route where you can keep moving; a stop with no pick is the one thing
that muddies this walk.

## Prediction 4 was measuring nothing

`session_mode` would have read `"unresolved"` on **every** walk. Sequence:

```
finishCleanup()  ->  setIsListening(false)
keep-awake effect ->  setSessionMode(null)      <- reset fires here
Save tapped      ->  saveSummary() reads state  <- always null by now
```

The state is cleared at the end of the walk, before the summary sheet's Save
reads it. Fixed by holding the mode in a ref set when it resolves and cleared
only at the START of the next walk. So the screen-off change is still unverified
in the field — the instrument was broken, not necessarily the change.

**Still open from this round:** B7, the isolated-picks walk. That is the one
that decides the segmentation fix.

## Every count maps to a real pick

Reconstructing the pick schedule from "12 picks, ~20s apart, 4 minutes", each
counted event falls within 6 seconds of an expected pick and **no counted event
is left over**:

```
~25 -> t=29 (+4)   ~45 -> t=41 (-4)   ~65 -> t=69 (+4)   ~85 -> t=85 (0)
~105 -> t=109 (+4) ~125 -> MISSED     ~145 -> t=148 (+3) ~165 -> t=171 (+6)
~185 -> t=189 (+4) ~205 -> t=208 (+3) ~225 -> t=225 (0)  ~245 -> MISSED
```

**The two misses:**

1. **~t=125** — `still at own pace: 1.20 m/s vs 1.26 median`. The pace gate fired
   because GPS had not caught the stop. A genuine recall cost and the next thing
   to look at.
2. **~t=245** — the log ends at t=243 on a 4-minute walk, so pick 12 almost
   certainly landed inside `trimRecentPickups(6000)`. **Protocol, not detector.**
   If so, true recall is 10 of 11 = 91%.

## Caveat on the fit

Pick times were **reconstructed, not logged** — the expected grid is built from
the stated protocol. The fit is strong (10 of 12 within ±6s, phase the only free
parameter) and far better constrained than the cluster-threshold reasoning
retracted on 24 Aug, but it is still inference. The LOG PICK button in Build 32
converts it to measurement.

## Prediction 4 finally answered

`session_mode: "background"` — the ref fix works, and the screen-off change is
confirmed in the field for the first time. The screen slept for the whole walk.

## What produced the change

`isNotStriding` reading the `speedMps` argument it had always been passed and
never used, plus reverting the 24 Aug walking-context refresh. That gave the
detector back the ability to recognise a two-second pick-pause, which every
stop-protection guard had been silently missing.
